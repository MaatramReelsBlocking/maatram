In detail

Cutting screen time without going cold turkey

By Team Maatram, the five class 10B students at SSVM School of Excellence who build and use Maatram · Updated 17 September 2026

Maatram is a free screen-time and focus web app for students. Five class 10B students at SSVM School of Excellence in Coimbatore, India built it. It puts six tools on one points balance: Focus Timers, App Gate, Study Room, Screen Stats, Leaderboard and Wellness.

Deleting the apps works for about two days. Maatram takes the other route: it keeps the apps where they are and puts a cost, a pause and an audience between you and the feed. Every tool on this site feeds one points balance, so a focused hour and a lost hour both show up in the same number.

App Gate blocks the apps that eat the evening

App Gate is the hard-lock screen. Pick a window between 5 and 90 minutes and Instagram, YouTube, Snapchat, TikTok and the rest stay shut for the whole run. Break the lock early and the points come off your balance instead of a lecture appearing. On the Android build the same lock is enforced outside the browser by Maatram Shield, so switching apps does not switch off the rule.

Study Room turns focus into something four other people can see

A Study Room holds five students on one room code with their timers synced live. Everyone in the room sees who opened which app, and three strikes puts you out of the session. There is a chat in the room so people can say something before the third strike lands. Rooms survive a page change, so walking to App Gate and back does not drop your seat.

Focus Timers and Screen Stats keep the score honest

Focus Timers run Pomodoro at 25 and 5 minutes and Deep Work at 90 and 15. Finishing a session pays points and extends the streak. Screen Stats is where you log daily per-app screen time by hand and watch the week take shape as a chart; land under your own weekly average and the bonus lands automatically. The Leaderboard ranks real signed-in students only, so the list starts empty and fills as your friends join.

Getting started

Who it is for, and how to start

Maatram opens with a Google sign-in, and the first thing it asks is who you are: student, teacher or parent. A teacher creates a class and gets a six-character code. Students join with that code. A parent joins with the same code and then picks their child off the class roster, so the three views stay connected without anyone hunting for accounts. Nothing on the site needs an install to try — the Android build is there for people who want the lock enforced outside the browser.

One points balance, no separate scores

There is a single number behind everything. Finishing a Pomodoro pays 25 and a Deep Work block pays 100. Ten minutes in a Study Room pays 10. Breaking an App Gate lock takes points off. Come in under your own weekly screen-time average and the weekly bonus lands on the same balance. That balance is what the Leaderboard sorts on, so the ranking reflects work done rather than time logged in.

What is stored, and what is not

Screen-time entries on the Stats page are yours to type in, and your daily numbers stay in that browser. The Leaderboard shows points, not hours, so nobody else sees what you use your phone for. Full detail lives on the privacy page, and the terms page covers the rest.

Beyond focus: the Sports corner

Screen time is not only a study problem, so the site also carries a Sports corner for what happens once the phone is down. It sits alongside the focus tools rather than replacing them, and it uses the same account.

Frequently asked questions

Is Maatram free?

Yes. Maatram is completely free and open source. There are no ads, no paid tier and no subscription.

How does Maatram reduce screen time?

Maatram puts friction between you and distracting apps. The App Gate makes you wait and pay a points toll before opening social media, Hard Lock blocks chosen apps for 5 to 90 minutes, and focus timers plus study rooms reward the time you spend off your phone.

Do I need to create an account?

For the focus tools, yes. Timers, App Gate, Study Room, Screen Stats, Leaderboard and the Sports corner open after a Google sign-in and a one-tap student, teacher or parent choice, because points and classes sync to that account. The home page, Wellness, Socials, Updates and the Android download page work without signing in.

Where is my screen-time data stored?

Day-by-day usage data is stored locally in your own browser. Only your productivity points and profile are synced when you choose to sign in.
